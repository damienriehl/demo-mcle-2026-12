# Community Water Ledger Bill — Proposed Committee Substitute

Be it enacted by the Legislature of the State of Northmere: this fictional demonstration bill establishes a community water ledger program. The Northmere Water Ledger Office (Office) shall administer the program, and the Kestrivane Cooperative Water District (District) shall serve as the initial participating provider. These invented institutions and this proposed enactment are used solely to demonstrate comparison of legislative drafts.

## Section 1. Purpose and Definitions

The program shall make neighborhood water-use information understandable without publishing individual household histories. In this Act, a participating provider means a local water supplier approved by the Office. A ledger means a monthly public statement of aggregate use and infrastructure losses. A household record means information reasonably capable of linking use to an occupied dwelling. The District is subject to the same protections and duties as any subsequently approved provider.

## Section 2. Initial Participation

The District shall join the program within one hundred eighty days after the effective date. Other providers may apply by submitting a service-area description and a proposed reporting contact. The Office shall approve an application that demonstrates the ability to produce accurate aggregate reports and protect household records. A provider's participation shall not change its service boundaries, rate-setting authority, or duties to maintain reliable drinking-water service.

## Section 3. Public Ledger

Each participating provider shall publish a ledger every calendar month. The ledger shall state total supplied volume, estimated infrastructure losses, and the method used to calculate those losses. Figures shall be accompanied by a plain-language explanation of limitations. The Office shall provide a common form and permit correction of a clerical error by a dated replacement that preserves access to the original explanation of the calculation.

## Section 4. Household Protections

A provider shall aggregate published data into groups of at least twenty occupied dwellings and suppress a value that would identify a particular household's use. The Office may inspect underlying records solely to verify compliance with this Act. Neither participation nor receipt of a grant authorizes disclosure of a household record to the public. A resident may request correction of a misattributed address through the provider's existing customer process.

## Section 5. Implementation Grants

The Office shall reimburse approved implementation expenses up to $60,000 per provider, subject to the appropriation in Section 9. Eligible expenses include meter-data reconciliation, accessible publication tools, and staff instruction. Ordinary water production costs are ineligible. A provider shall document the expense and certify that no other public grant has reimbursed the same cost. Approval of participation does not itself promise a particular grant amount.

## Section 6. Verification and Correction

The Office may examine a provider's program records upon twenty business days' written notice. A written finding shall identify the disputed entry and the basis for correction. The provider shall have ten days to correct a substantiated reporting deficiency. A correction shall disclose its date and reason without republishing protected household information. The Office shall close a finding when the provider supplies records demonstrating the deficiency has been corrected.

## Section 7. Administrative Assessments

After notice and an opportunity for an administrative hearing, the Office may assess up to $100 for each month of continued failure to correct a substantiated deficiency. An assessment shall consider the provider's size, good-faith correction efforts, and the practical availability of source records. The provider may contest the assessment under the State of Northmere's fictional Administrative Review Act. No assessment may be imposed solely because an estimate later proves inaccurate.

## Section 8. Annual Review

The Office shall publish an annual program review describing participation, reporting completeness, implementation expenses, and recurring calculation problems. The review shall use aggregated information and recommend whether program forms require revision. Each participating provider may submit a response for inclusion as an appendix. The Office shall distinguish provider-reported estimates from independently verified measurements and explain any material difference in methodology across reporting periods.

A participating provider shall retain the worksheets supporting each public ledger for three years after publication. The Office may request those worksheets for program oversight, but any household identifiers shall remain subject to the disclosure restrictions established by this Act.

## Section 9. Appropriation

For the first fiscal year of operation, $900,000 is appropriated to the Office from the fictional Community Infrastructure Fund for implementation grants and program administration. The Office shall publish aggregate grant awards and administrative expenses separately. Uncommitted funds revert to that fund at the close of the following fiscal year. This section does not authorize borrowing, a new household charge, or a transfer from provider operating reserves.

## Section 10. Effective Date and Construction

This Act takes effect ninety days after enactment. The Office may prepare forms before that date but may not require an earlier submission. References to days mean calendar days unless business days are expressly specified. If a provision is invalid, the remaining provisions continue to operate to the extent practicable. This Act shall be known as the Community Water Ledger Act of the State of Northmere.
