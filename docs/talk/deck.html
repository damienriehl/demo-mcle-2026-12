§ 2.1 · Radar, Cockpit, PortalThree views, one store

Every deadline, every matter, one glance.

  • Deadline Radar shows every deadline across ten matters, with a "This week" band on top.
  • Cockpit opens one matter in depth: parties, timeline, documents, and tabs.
  • Client Portal shows the client the same matter, minus our internal reasoning.
One edit, three screens

Change a trigger date and the Radar, the Cockpit, and the Portal update live.

We store trigger dates, never due dates. Code computes every due date on read.

Seven practice areas: litigation, transactional, patent, bankruptcy, family, employment, and regulatory.5
§ 2.2 · Ramble intakeSpeak a matter

Ramble for a minute. Get a structured matter.

  • Talk the way you would to a paralegal. The model returns a matter that passes the schema.
  • Embellish fills realistic gaps and marks each one invented.
  • Show what I said puts your words next to each field.
Every field carries provenance
StatedYou said it.
InferredThe model reasoned to it from your words.
InventedEmbellish made it up, and the field says so.
Ramble to matter: about 10 seconds against the live model.6
§ 2.3 · Trust but VerifyShow your math

Code counts the days. The rule speaks for itself.

  • Open any deadline and see each step of the math, with the rule's own words.
  • Flip service from electronic to mail and watch Rule 6(d) add three days.
  • Federal and Minnesota rules: FRCP 6(a), 6(d), and Gen. R. Prac. 115.03.
Interrogatory answers, served by mail

The model never computes a deadline. A tested rules engine does.7
§ 2.4 · Scheduling ordersDrop a PDF

Drop the order. Every date lands, with its quote.

  • The model copies each court-set date and the sentence that sets it.
  • The server checks every quote against the page before it saves anything.
  • Code derives the rule deadlines, then exports a calendar file for Outlook or Google.

§ 2.6 · Draft CompareTwo drafts in, advice out

A redline that knows whose side you're on.

  • Compare Word, PDF, text, Markdown, .doc, .rtf, .odt, or HTML.
  • Get Word-faithful tracked changes, with moved clauses linked.
  • Name your client. Each change gets advice: Harms, Neutral, or Helps.
  • Download a redline with margin comments, plus a counter-draft in your own tracked changes.
Seller's aggregate liability shall not exceed the Purchase Price ten percent (10%) of the Purchase Price.
Harms  for the buyer

The cap drops from the full Purchase Price to 10%. Counter: "shall not exceed the Purchase Price."

Concepts tagged from the open FOLIO legal ontology. Every quote verified against the draft.10
§ 3 · GuardrailsTrust, then verify in code

Never trust the model's word. Check it on the server.

  • Synthetic clients only. Real orders stay public records.
  • Every quote gets matched against its source page.
  • Code, never the model, computes deadlines.
  • The model labels concepts only by index into a fixed ontology.
  • The app listens only on this laptop, never the open network.
  • The API key never appears on screen or in a log.
Same duties as always: competence, confidentiality, and supervision. New tools.11
